\section{Word recovery can succeed where every scalar frame is singular}
Let $V$ be a rational vector space of target Hodge classes and let
$\mathrm{cl}:C\to W$ be the supplied cycle map, with $j:V\to W$ its
cohomological inclusion. Suppose independently constructed source operations
give maps $L_i:V\to C$ and linear $F_i:V\to V$ satisfying
$\mathrm{cl}(L_i(v))=j(F_i v)$. The $L_i$ need not be linear.
For a nonempty word $w=(i_1,\ldots,i_s)$ define
\[
 Z_w(\alpha)=L_{i_1}(F_{i_2}\cdots F_{i_s}\alpha).
\]
This does not require a native endomorphism on $C$ for the tail of the word;
the tail is evaluated on the target Hodge fiber and then read into a solved
source. Naturality gives its entire class.
\begin{theorem}[Target-first word reconstruction]
If a finite relation $\sum_w a_w F_w\alpha=\alpha$ holds, then
$\sum_w a_w Z_w(\alpha)$ is an actual cycle with class $j(\alpha)$.
An empty word is excluded: otherwise the purported construction would
silently request the target's own cycle.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Apply the cycle map and the displayed source identities, then linearity.
Every resulting summand is $a_w j(F_w\alpha)$.
\end{proof}
Existing branch noncommutative frames already evaluate words on an algebraic
spine. The distinction here is target-first source readout, no native tail
operator, and the following explicit certificate rather than an assumed
full-rank matrix of word outputs.

Put
\[
 A=\begin{pmatrix}0&0&0\\0&0&-1\\0&1&0\end{pmatrix},\quad
 B=\begin{pmatrix}0&0&1\\0&0&0\\-1&0&0\end{pmatrix},\quad
 C=\begin{pmatrix}0&-1&0\\1&0&0\\0&0&0\end{pmatrix}.
\]
\begin{theorem}[Singular linear family, exact word identity]
Over $\Q$ (and as polynomial matrix identities over $\Z$),
\[
 \det(xA+yB+zC)=0,\qquad A^2+B^2+C^2=-2I,
 \qquad A^2B^2+A^2C^2+B^2C^2=I.
\]
Thus no fixed scalar combination gives an invertible global source frame,
whereas three nonempty words recover every vector. The length-four formula
has integer coefficients, eliminating the half in the shorter quadratic
formula. No optimality of word length or denominator is asserted.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
The weighted matrix annihilates $(x,y,z)^t$; its determinant also expands
identically to zero, including $(x,y,z)=0$.
The squares are $\operatorname{diag}(0,-1,-1)$,
$\operatorname{diag}(-1,0,-1)$ and
$\operatorname{diag}(-1,-1,0)$.
Their three pair products are the three coordinate projectors.
All formulas follow without commuting $A,B,C$.
\end{proof}
For actual source operators obeying this word relation, the original-$X$
cycle is, explicitly,
$L_A(AB^2\alpha)+L_A(AC^2\alpha)+L_B(BC^2\alpha)$.
Existence of such source geometry for arbitrary $X$ is not asserted.

\section{The exact obstruction is larger than fixed vectors}
Let $N=1-F$ be the preceding update's residual operator.
\begin{theorem}[Generalized fixed-mode obstruction]
Suppose $F^r\alpha=0$ for some finite $r$ and
$q(N)\alpha=0$ for a rational polynomial with $q(1)\ne0$.
Then $\alpha=0$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Induct on $r$. The case $r=0$ is immediate. For the successor, $F$ commutes
with $q(N)$, so $F\alpha$ satisfies the same annihilator and has one shorter
nilpotence length. Induction gives $F\alpha=0$. Consequently $N\alpha=\alpha$
and $q(N)\alpha=q(1)\alpha=0$. Since $q(1)$ is nonzero, $\alpha=0$.
\end{proof}
In finite dimension, Fitting decomposition sharpens this: the largest
subspace on which a fixed-$N$ polynomial certificate can work is the stable
image of $F$, not necessarily its entire image. Indeed $V=\ker F^d\oplus
\operatorname{im}F^d$ for stabilized $d$; $F$ is invertible on the second
summand, whose residual minimal polynomial has no root at $1$. The theorem
excludes the first summand. This is a hand-proof characterization; the Lean
file encodes the universal generalized-mode obstruction without assuming
finite dimension.
For completeness choose $d$ with both kernels and images stabilized.
If $x=F^dy$ also satisfies $F^dx=0$, then $F^{2d}y=0$; kernel stabilization
gives $F^dy=0$, so $x=0$. Rank-nullity now makes the sum direct and exhaustive.
Image stabilization makes $F$ bijective on the stable image. The characteristic
polynomial of $N$ on that image annihilates it and has value
$\det(1-N)=\det F\ne0$ at $1$, giving the required polynomial certificate.
For example take $F=J_2(0)\oplus(2)\oplus(3)$.
The spectral projector and inverse on the nonzero spectrum are
\[
 E=F^2(19I-5F)/36,\qquad L=F^2(65I-19F)/216,
 \quad FL=LF=E.
\]
The first basis vector is in $\operatorname{im}F$ but lies in $\ker E$.
Its source cycle can be obtained from the second vector; no fixed-$N$
polynomial acting only on that first vector can recover it.
The stronger distinction already occurs for $F=J_3(0)$:
$e_2=Fe_3$ lies in the source image, $Fe_2=e_1\ne0$, and $F^2e_2=0$.
It is not a fixed vector of $N=1-F$, so the old fixed-vector guard alone
does not detect it. The generalized-mode theorem does.

\section{Arbitrary nilpotent depth with no commutativity}
In any associative unital ring define
$x\star y=x+y+xy$. Then
\[
 1+(x\star y)=(1+x)(1+y).
\]
\begin{theorem}[Ordered algebra-group dressing]
The operation $\star$ is associative with identity zero. If $x^n=0$, set
$S_n(x)=\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}(-x)^k$ and $x^{-\star}=S_n(x)-1$.
Then $(1+x)S_n(x)=S_n(x)(1+x)=1$ and
$x\star x^{-\star}=x^{-\star}\star x=0$.
There is no commutativity, characteristic-zero, or cubic-depth restriction.
For a nilpotent two-sided ideal all these coordinates stay in the ideal,
so its coordinates form a group.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Expand associativity in order; both sides are
$x+y+z+xy+xz+yz+xyz$.
The finite geometric series telescopes on each side, leaving
$1-(-x)^n=1$. Subtracting one from the inverse gives the coordinate inverse.
Closure and coordinate nilpotence follow from the two-sided ideal hypothesis.
\end{proof}
\begin{theorem}[Exact ordered gauge defect]
Whenever $(1+c)S=1$,
\[
 (1+a)(1+b)S-1=(a+b+ab-c)S.
\]
Consequently the exact primitive equation is $a\star b=c$.
An additive primitive $a+b=c$ leaves the explicit quadratic term $abS$;
it cannot be declared a nonabelian primitive without that correction.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Subtract $(1+c)S=1$ from $(1+a)(1+b)S$ and factor on the right.
\end{proof}
The preceding area obstruction is not contradicted: it prohibits an
additive homomorphic parametrization, not the ordered group law itself.

\section{Finite-group averaging: actual primitives with an explicit scale}
Let $G$ be a finite group of order $m$, acting additively on an abelian
group $M$. Let $c:G\times G\to M$ satisfy the actual cocycle equation
\[
 c(g,h)+c(gh,k)=g c(h,k)+c(g,hk).
\]
Define $S(g)=\sum_{k\in G}c(g,k)$.
\begin{theorem}[Universal finite averaging]
\[
 S(g)+gS(h)-S(gh)=m c(g,h).
\]
For a rational module, $b=S/m$ therefore satisfies $\delta b=c$.
For a normalized cocycle, $b(1)=0$. No commutative-group assumption on $G$
or trivial-action assumption on $M$ is needed.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Sum the cocycle equation over $k$. Left multiplication by $h$ permutes $G$,
so $\sum_k c(g,hk)=S(g)$. The action preserves the sum. Rearrangement proves
the integral identity; divide by $m\ne0$ over $\Q$.
\end{proof}
Suppose actual lifts $s_g$ in a possibly noncommutative group and an additive
embedding $i:M\to E$ obey
$s_gs_h=i(c(g,h))s_{gh}$ and
$s_g i(v)=i(gv)s_g$. Then $\widetilde s_g=i(-b(g))s_g$ satisfies
$\widetilde s_g\widetilde s_h=\widetilde s_{gh}$, by direct multiplication.
This corrects an abelian extension layer, and can be repeated through
a finite central filtration with rational vector-space quotients. Each
layer must actually have this form. General $\mathrm{SL}$-valued transport,
an infinite translation groupoid, or an unknown geometric deck does not
satisfy these hypotheses merely by being traceless.
Indeed $A=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}0&1\\1&0\end{smallmatrix}\right)$ is
traceless and $A^2=I$; none of its powers vanish. Conjugation or adjoining
zero blocks cannot turn it into a nilpotent operator.

\begin{theorem}[Iteration through a rational central filtration]
Let $1\to U\to E\to G\to1$ be an actual group extension with finite $G$.
Suppose $U=U_1\supseteq\cdots\supseteq U_{r+1}=1$ is an $E$-invariant
central filtration: $[U,U_j]\subseteq U_{j+1}$, and each $U_j/U_{j+1}$
is an additive rational vector space with rational-linear conjugation action.
Then the quotient map admits a group-theoretic section.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Begin with the identity section in $E/U=G$. Given a section into $E/U_j$,
choose lifts of its finitely many values into $E/U_{j+1}$.
Their multiplication defect is a $2$-cocycle in $U_j/U_{j+1}$; associativity
and injectivity of that kernel give its cocycle equation. Conjugation descends
to a $G$-action because $U$ acts trivially on this central quotient.
Finite averaging and the corrected-lift product formula remove this defect.
This constructs the next section. Iterate for $j=1,\ldots,r$.
This induction requires no commutativity of $U$, but does require every
displayed rational central quotient. The Lean file formalizes the averaging
and corrected-product step; no automatic geometric filtration is asserted.
\end{proof}

\section{Arbitrary-X source attempt and decisive geometric boundary}
Attempt: take the prescribed $\alpha$'s carrier, correct finite holonomy
layer by layer, and use the corrected unipotent object as a fresh Chern source.
The averaging and ordered formulas solve the algebraic layers in their
stated domain. But the proposed source has the following precise limitation.
If on a genuine degree-$m$ finite cover $f:Y\to X$ its algebraic bundle
has a filtration whose quotients are trivial, then
$[f^*E]=\operatorname{rank}(E)[\mathcal O_Y]$ in algebraic $K_0(Y)$.
Hence $f^*\operatorname{ch}_p(E)=0$ for every $p>0$.
The canonical push-pull equation gives
$m\operatorname{ch}_p(E)=f_*f^*\operatorname{ch}_p(E)=0$ in rational Chow,
so $\operatorname{ch}_p(E)=0$ on the original $X$.
More generally extensions do not change the sum of their graded $K_0$
classes. Thus coherence correction can preserve a previously supplied
source but cannot manufacture the prescribed nonzero primitive class from
trivial graded pieces. Nontrivial graded sources, or a different support
constructor, remain essential. This is a restriction on this executed
constructor, not a refutation of GST or of the Hodge conjecture.
