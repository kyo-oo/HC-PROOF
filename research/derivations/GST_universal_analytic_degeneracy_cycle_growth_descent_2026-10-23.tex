\section{Polynomial growth of the projector IS finite-pole algebraic geometry}
Fix ANY closed algebraic embedding of the actual
smooth affine J into affine complex space
$J\hookrightarrow\mathbb A_{\mathbb C}^K$,
and denote the coordinate vector by $z$.
A holomorphic function f on J^{an} is
\emph{polynomially bounded} if
there exist C>0 and k>=0 with
\[
 \boxed{|f(z)|\le C(1+\|z\|)^k\quad
                       \text{for every }z\in J^{an}.}     \tag{4.1}
\]
The condition is equivalent for any pair
of affine algebraic embeddings (possibly
with a different exponent) because the
coordinate maps are regular polynomials.
This is a quantitative bound on genuine
growth at infinity of the folded geometry.

\begin{lemma}[Polynomial-growth Liouville principle on algebraic Stein folds]
Let J be any smooth affine algebraic variety
over C and f a holomorphic function on J^{an}.
Then
\[
 \boxed{
 f\in\mathcal O_{\mathrm{alg}}(J)
 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 f\text{ satisfies the finite polynomial bound (4.1)}.
 }                                                       \tag{4.2}
\]
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
A regular function on an affine closed embedding
is the restriction of a polynomial in the ambient
coordinates, so satisfies (4.1). Conversely,
choose a smooth projective compactification
$\overline J$ of J with a simple-normal-crossing
divisor B as boundary. Each ambient affine
coordinate z_i extends to a rational function
on $\overline J$, hence has at worst finite
poles along the finitely many divisorial
components of B. In a local coordinate
polydisc meeting B with local defining
coordinates $(t_1,\dots,t_\ell)$, their
pole growth is bounded by
$C'\prod_j|t_j|^{-a_j}$ for finite exponents.
The polynomial growth assumption for f
then supplies finite exponents b_j such that
$|\prod_j t_j^{b_j}f|\le C''$ near B.
The removable-singularity theorem across
normal crossing divisors extends this
product holomorphically. Thus f extends
meromorphically to $\overline J$, with
finite poles supported on B. A meromorphic
function on smooth complex projective
$\overline J$ is rational by GAGA;
as f has no poles on smooth J, that
rational function is regular there.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Uniform finite spatial-pole criterion at fixed X,p]
The following are equivalent:

(i) Every rational $(p,p)$ class on X

algebraic cycle.

(ii) There exists ONE finite integer

This K does not depend on alpha, its
rational denominator, or the necessary
matrix rank/dimension.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
(ii)->(i) follows from the previous theorem.
For (i)->(ii), choose a finite rational
basis alpha_1,...,alpha_h of the rational
Hodge fiber. For each alpha_i,
the previous proof gives a difference
of two finite projective algebraic modules,
with full pure Chern-character difference
m_i q^*alpha_i, represented by finite
idempotents with some pole bounds k_i.
Let K be the maximum of these finitely
many bounds and of the bound needed for
one algebraic free complement of their
negative summands. By enlarging the
individual module presentations finitely
many times, each basis direction can be
represented by a SINGLE positive
algebraic projective module whose
full positive Chern character is rank
plus m_i alpha_i and whose idempotent
has some finite bound k_i'.
For an arbitrary rational linear combination
of the alpha_i, clear a common denominator.
Positive multiples can be implemented
by block-diagonal direct sums of these
modules; negative multiples require a
representative for -alpha_i, which is
likewise constructed once for each i
via finite projective-module
complements. Choose K as the maximum
of all these finite basis and negated
basis idempotent bounds. Block-diagonal
sums preserve the largest individual
pole order, while their ranks may grow.
This gives (ii).
\end{proof}
\begin{remark}
The uniform bound K is an exact

The point is that a SINGLE finite

\end{remark}

\section{Exact two non-shortcuts: no holomorphic section and boundedness}
\begin{theorem}[No global holomorphic section of the affine spatial fold]
If X has positive complex dimension,
$q:J_N(X)^{an}\to X^{an}$ has NO
global holomorphic section.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Any holomorphic map from a connected

to J existed with q s=id_X,

\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Zero-growth analytic projectors are constant]
Every bounded holomorphic function on
a smooth affine algebraic J is constant.
Consequently any finite holomorphic
idempotent matrix P whose entries
satisfy growth exponent k=0 is

\begin{proof}

HOLomorphic (not merely meromorphic)

has no poles. Thus f extends to
the connected compact projective
compactification and is constant
by the maximum principle.
A constant idempotent gives a
trivial finite-dimensional subspace
of a trivial bundle; its positive
Chern character vanishes.
\end{proof}
